# First-order terms

**Claim:** from his physics training: every system has a first-order approximation that explains most of it; find it, show it working, then add higher-order terms. Feeds [rule 2](../rules.md).

> I always try to find the first-order terms or the second-order terms of everything.
> — `raw/interviews/dwarkesh-2025.md`

> How can I have a simplest thing that shows that thing, shows it in action, and then I can tack on the other terms?
> — `raw/interviews/dwarkesh-2025.md`

> When a physicist walks into the class and they say, “Assume there’s a spherical cow,” everyone laughs at that, but this is brilliant.
> — `raw/interviews/dwarkesh-2025.md`

In engineering form this is the [recipe](../methods/recipe-for-training.md): simplest model you can't have screwed up, then “complexify only one at a time”. In teaching form it's the [ramp](../methods/ramps-to-knowledge.md): the GPT lecture starts from a bigram lookup table.
