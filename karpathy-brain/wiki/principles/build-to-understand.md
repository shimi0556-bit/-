# Build to understand

**Claim:** you only understand what you can build; building exposes the gaps you didn't know you had. Feeds [rule 1](../rules.md).

> when you build something from scratch, you’re forced to come to terms with what you don’t understand and you don’t know that you don’t understand it.
> — `raw/interviews/dwarkesh-2025.md`

> You’re not allowed to copy-paste, you’re allowed to reference, you’re not allowed to copy-paste.
> — `raw/interviews/dwarkesh-2025.md`

> And in the spirit of “what I cannot create I do not understand”, what better way to do this than implement it from scratch?
> — `raw/blog/2021-06-21-blockchain.md`

How it shows up: nearly every repo is a from-scratch rebuild of something that already exists ([micrograd](../sources/micrograd.md), [minbpe](../sources/minbpe.md), [cryptos](../sources/cryptos.md), [llm.c](../sources/llm-c.md), [microgpt](../sources/microgpt.md)); his advice for studying nanochat is to retype it with the reference on the other monitor ([interview](../sources/dwarkesh-2025.md)).

**Tension:** he also coined vibe coding and happily uses agents for code he doesn't need to understand ([coding with LLMs](../methods/coding-with-llms.md)). The resolution in his own words is about stakes: he vibe-codes boilerplate and the parts he can test against a version he fully understands.
