# Non-Customer Analysis: The Three Tiers

## Why Non-Customers Matter

In most industries, non-customers outnumber customers by a significant margin. The hotel industry serves millions, but billions travel without using traditional hotels. The car industry sells hundreds of millions, but billions of people don't own a car.

Traditional innovation focuses on making existing customers happier. Blue Ocean Strategy asks a more powerful question: **Why don't non-customers buy, and what would it take to convert them?**

Converting even a fraction of non-customers often represents a larger opportunity than winning market share from competitors.

## The Three Tiers

### Tier 1: Soon-to-Be Non-Customers

**Who they are:** People who currently use the industry's offerings but minimally and reluctantly. They're on the edge of the market, one better alternative away from leaving.

**Characteristics:**
- Use the product out of necessity, not satisfaction
- Actively seeking alternatives
- Complain frequently or use the product differently than intended
- First to switch when a substitute appears
- Their usage is declining over time

**How to identify them:**
- Customer surveys with low satisfaction scores
- Users with declining usage patterns
- Customers who use only a fraction of features
- Customers on the cheapest plan or smallest purchase size
- Support tickets asking "can I do X differently?"

**What to investigate:**
- What are they tolerating? (Pain points they endure)
- What would make them leave? (Tipping point)
- What aspects do they value vs. which feel forced on them?
- What workarounds have they created?

**Conversion approach:** Remove what they hate, amplify what they value. Often means simplification.

**Example (Budget Hotels):**
Business travelers who use budget hotels reluctantly—they hate the poor sleep quality but can't justify premium hotel costs. They'd switch to anything that offered a great bed at a fair price, even if everything else was minimal.

### Tier 2: Refusing Non-Customers

**Who they are:** People who have actively evaluated the industry's offerings and consciously decided NOT to buy. They're aware of the products but have rejected them for specific reasons.

**Characteristics:**
- Made a deliberate choice not to use the product
- Have specific, articulable reasons for refusal
- Often use substitutes from other industries
- Their refusal is rational (to them), not just uninformed
- May have tried the product once and rejected it

**How to identify them:**
- Market research on "why didn't you buy?"
- People using substitute products/services
- People who tried a free trial but didn't convert
- Forums/communities discussing alternatives
- People who explicitly say "that's not for me"

**What to investigate:**
- What is their specific dealbreaker?
- What substitute are they using instead?
- What would need to change for them to reconsider?
- Is the barrier price, complexity, relevance, access, or identity?
- Do they represent a pattern (many people refusing for the same reason)?

**Conversion approach:** Address the specific dealbreaker. Often means fundamentally reimagining what the product is.

**Example (Car industry):**
Scooter riders in Southeast Asia who have evaluated cars and decided they're too expensive, require too much parking hassle, and aren't practical for narrow roads. Converting them requires rethinking what "personal transportation" means—not just making a cheaper car.

### Tier 3: Unexplored Non-Customers

**Who they are:** People in markets or contexts that the industry has never targeted. The industry doesn't see them as potential customers. They may not even know the product category exists, or they assume it's not for them.

**Characteristics:**
- The industry has never marketed to them
- They don't see themselves as part of the target market
- Demographic, geographic, or cultural distance from current customers
- May have needs the industry doesn't recognize
- Often the largest tier by far

**How to identify them:**
- Ask: "Who would NEVER consider this product? Why?"
- Look at demographics the industry ignores (age, income, geography)
- Consider markets in different countries/cultures
- Think about use cases the industry hasn't imagined
- Look for people solving the underlying need in completely different ways

**What to investigate:**
- What assumptions make the industry blind to this group?
- What is their version of the underlying need?
- What would the product need to look like to serve them?
- What infrastructure, knowledge, or access barriers exist?
- Could serving them reveal a much larger market?

**Conversion approach:** Redefine the product category entirely. Often requires creating something the industry wouldn't recognize as a competing product.

**Example (Car industry):**
Teenagers (15-year-olds) who can't drive legally but need personal transportation. The entire car industry ignores them. But what if "personal transportation" included electric scooters, autonomous vehicles, or subscription ride services designed specifically for teens? This is a huge demographic that the auto industry has zero strategy for.

## Cross-Tier Analysis

After mapping all three tiers, look for patterns:

### Shared Barriers
What's the common thread? If all three tiers cite complexity as a barrier (Tier 1 hates the learning curve, Tier 2 rejected because of complexity, Tier 3 is intimidated by it), then simplification is your Blue Ocean strategy.

### Shared Unmet Needs
What do all three tiers want that nobody provides? This often points to the "Create" factor in your ERRC grid.

### Size Estimation
Which tier is largest? Usually Tier 3, but it's also the hardest to convert. Tier 1 is easiest to convert but may be smallest. Tier 2 is often the sweet spot—large enough to be meaningful, specific enough to target.

## Global Diversity in Non-Customer Analysis

Non-customers are NOT the same across markets. The same industry has fundamentally different non-customer profiles in different regions.

### Example: Personal Finance/Banking

**US Tier 2:** People who refuse traditional banking because of fees, minimum balances, and distrust after 2008 crisis. Use fintech apps instead.

**Nigeria Tier 3:** Unbanked market traders who've never had a bank account. No ID documents, no branch nearby, no minimum balance to meet. Use cash and mobile money.

**Japan Tier 1:** Elderly people who have bank accounts but are intimidated by online banking. Use passbook savings at the branch window. Reluctant, declining users.

**India Tier 2:** Small farmers who refuse formal banking because crop income is seasonal and unpredictable. Microfinance terms don't match their cash flow. Use informal village lending.

**Each requires a completely different Blue Ocean strategy.**

### How to Apply Global Diversity

1. For each tier, consider at least 2 different geographic/cultural contexts
2. Note how barriers differ (infrastructure vs. price vs. cultural vs. regulatory)
3. Identify if ONE strategy could serve non-customers across multiple markets, or if different markets need different approaches
4. Use authentic, specific examples (not "developing country users")

## From Non-Customer Analysis to Four Actions

Non-customer insights directly feed the ERRC grid:

**Tier 1 complaints → ELIMINATE/REDUCE candidates**
"They hate X" → Can we eliminate or reduce X?

**Tier 2 dealbreakers → RAISE/CREATE candidates**
"They rejected because of Y" → Can we raise the standard on Y or create something that eliminates the objection?

**Tier 3 barriers → CREATE candidates**
"They've never been targeted because of Z assumption" → What new factor breaks that assumption?

## Practical Tips

1. **Interview non-customers when possible.** Even 5-10 conversations reveal patterns that no amount of desk research can match.

2. **Ask "why not?" five times.** Surface-level reasons ("too expensive") often mask deeper barriers ("I don't trust it" or "it's not designed for people like me").

3. **Map substitutes carefully.** What non-customers use INSTEAD is as important as why they don't use your offering. Substitutes reveal the real job-to-be-done.

4. **Don't assume you need ALL non-customers.** Converting 10% of Tier 2 may be enough to create a massive business. You don't need to boil the ocean.

5. **Tier 3 is where unicorns hide.** The most transformative Blue Oceans come from serving people the industry never imagined as customers. Push your thinking here.
