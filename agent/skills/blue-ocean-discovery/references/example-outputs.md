# Blue Ocean Discovery: Example Outputs

## Example 1: Budget Hospitality (Inspired by Accor Formula 1)

### Strategy Canvas

**Competitive Factors:**
1. Room price
2. Room comfort (bed quality, quiet)
3. Room aesthetics (decor, design)
4. Food & beverage (restaurant, minibar)
5. Lobby & common areas
6. Reception staff & concierge
7. Location (city center proximity)
8. Amenities (pool, gym, spa)
9. Check-in/check-out speed
10. Cleanliness
11. Brand prestige
12. Loyalty program

**Competitor Value Curves:**

| Factor | Luxury (Marriott) | Mid-range (Holiday Inn) | Budget (Motel 6) | Industry Avg |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Room price | 1 (high price) | 3 | 5 (low price) | 3 |
| Room comfort | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3.3 |
| Room aesthetics | 5 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| Food & beverage | 5 | 4 | 1 | 3.3 |
| Lobby & common areas | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3.3 |
| Reception staff | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| Location | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| Amenities | 5 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| Check-in speed | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2.3 |
| Cleanliness | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3.3 |
| Brand prestige | 5 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| Loyalty program | 5 | 4 | 2 | 3.7 |

**Observations:**
- **Convergence:** All tiers invest in reception staff (4+). Everyone has some lobby. Everyone has F&B.
- **Overinvestment:** Mid-range overinvests in amenities and F&B that budget travelers don't use.
- **Underinvestment:** Check-in speed is low across ALL tiers. Nobody prioritizes fast check-in.
- **Assumption:** The industry assumes hospitality requires human-staffed reception, F&B, and decorative common areas.

---

### Non-Customer Analysis

**Tier 1: Reluctant Budget Travelers**
- Road-tripping families who hate motel quality but can't justify $200/night hotels
- Sleep poorly, wake up unrested, view accommodation as a necessary evil
- Would pay slightly more for a genuinely good night's sleep, nothing else
- **Size:** Millions of domestic travelers who book the cheapest option resentfully

**Tier 2: Non-Hotel Travelers**
- Long-haul truck drivers sleeping in cabs because all hotels feel overpriced for a few hours of sleep
- Backpackers using hostels because hotels of ANY tier cost too much
- Conference attendees who drive home after events rather than pay for a room
- **Barrier:** Even budget hotels cost too much for "I just need to sleep for 6 hours"
- **Size:** Enormous—anyone who has ever driven through the night to avoid hotel costs

**Tier 3: Non-Travelers**
- People who avoid trips requiring overnight stays because of accommodation costs
- Elderly people who won't travel because hotels are confusing (check-in process, unfamiliar systems)
- Families in emerging markets who vacation at relatives' homes because no hotel is affordable
- **Barrier:** Hotels are designed for business/leisure travelers with expense accounts
- **Size:** Vast—billions globally who would travel more if accommodation cost less

**Cross-Tier Commonalities:**
- All tiers want a clean, quiet place to sleep
- None of them want restaurants, lobbies, or concierge services
- Price is the universal barrier
- Speed and simplicity are universally valued

---

### Four Actions Framework

**ELIMINATE:**
- Restaurant/F&B: Adds 15-20% to operating cost, budget travelers eat elsewhere
- Decorative lobby: Purely aesthetic, no functional value for "I just need a bed"
- Large reception staff: 3-shift staffing is expensive, check-in takes 5 minutes
- Room decor/aesthetics: Budget travelers don't choose based on room design
- Amenities (pool, gym): Budget travelers never use them, maintenance is costly

**REDUCE:**
- Room size: Sleep doesn't require a large room. Reduce to bed + bathroom
- Furniture: Desk and chair? Most budget travelers never use them. One surface.
- Loyalty program: Simple stamp card instead of complex points system

**RAISE:**
- Bed quality: Invest significantly in mattress/bedding. This is the ONE thing that matters.
- Cleanliness: Hospital-grade. Non-negotiable.
- Sound insulation: Quiet = good sleep. Worth investing in.
- Check-in speed: From 10 minutes to 60 seconds.

**CREATE:**
- Self-service kiosk check-in (like ATM—24/7, no staff needed)
- Modular rooms (pre-fabricated, identical, easy to clean/maintain)
- Ultra-transparent pricing (one price, no hidden fees, no "resort fee")

---

### New Value Curve

| Factor | Industry Avg | Blue Ocean (Formula 1 style) |
|--------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Room price | 3 | 5 (very low price) |
| Room comfort (bed) | 3.3 | 5 (excellent bed) |
| Room aesthetics | 3 | 1 (minimal) |
| Food & beverage | 3.3 | 0 (eliminated) |
| Lobby | 3.3 | 0 (eliminated) |
| Reception staff | 4 | 1 (minimal/automated) |
| Amenities | 3 | 0 (eliminated) |
| Check-in speed | 2.3 | 5 (60-second kiosk) |
| Cleanliness | 3.3 | 5 (exceptional) |
| Self-service kiosk | 0 | 5 (created) |
| Transparent pricing | 0 | 5 (created) |

**Result:** 20% lower operating cost than budget competitors. Dramatically better sleep experience. Served non-customers who previously couldn't justify any hotel. Formula 1 achieved 50% market share growth.

## Example 2: Mobile Financial Services (West Africa)

### Strategy Canvas

**Competitive Factors for Banking in Nigeria:**
1. Branch network size
2. Account minimum balance
3. Documentation requirements (KYC)
4. Interest on savings
5. Loan availability
6. Mobile app quality
7. Transaction fees
8. Customer service (phone/in-person)
9. ATM network
10. Product range (credit cards, insurance, etc.)

**Competitor Curves (abbreviated):**

| Factor | Traditional Bank | Microfinance | Mobile Money (OPay) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Branch network | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| Min balance | 1 (high barrier) | 3 | 5 (none) |
| Documentation | 1 (heavy) | 3 | 4 (light) |
| Loan availability | 3 | 4 | 2 |
| Mobile app | 3 | 1 | 5 |
| Transaction fees | 2 (high) | 3 | 4 (low) |
| Product range | 5 | 2 | 2 |

**Observations:**
- Banks over-invest in branch networks and product range
- All players require some documentation
- Nobody optimizes for people without smartphones
- Loan availability is moderate across the board

---

### Non-Customer Analysis

**Tier 1: Reluctant Bank Users**
- Market traders who have bank accounts but use them only for receiving payments from formal businesses
- Hate the minimum balance requirement, the fees, the long queues at branches
- Would leave if they had a simpler alternative for their daily business needs

**Tier 2: Consciously Unbanked**
- Traders and small business owners who've decided banks aren't for them
- Reasons: "Too many documents," "They don't understand my business," "Fees eat my profit"
- Use cash, market associations (ajo/esusu), and trusted individuals instead
- **This is the LARGEST tier in Nigeria—over 40 million adults**

**Tier 3: Never-Considered**
- Rural market women who've never interacted with any financial institution
- Illiterate or semi-literate, use feature phones, conduct all business in local language
- The concept of "an account" is foreign—they think in terms of physical cash
- Use tin-under-the-mattress savings and community contributions
- **Barrier:** Every financial product assumes literacy, smartphones, and English

**Cross-Tier Commonalities:**
- Documentation is universally hated/feared
- Everyone uses mobile phones (feature phones, not smartphones)
- Everyone understands mobile money transfers
- Trust comes from community, not institutions
- Cash flow is daily, not monthly

---

### Four Actions Framework

**ELIMINATE:**
- Branch network: Non-customers don't go to branches anyway
- Minimum balance: Immediate barrier for low-income users
- Complex documentation: Major friction point, especially for illiterate users
- Product range: Non-customers need ONE thing (safe money movement), not insurance and credit cards

**REDUCE:**
- Smartphone dependency: Reduce to USSD/SMS on feature phones
- English language dependency: Reduce by offering Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa
- Transaction fees: Reduce to minimal (revenue from volume, not per-transaction)

**RAISE:**
- Speed: From bank-queue hours to instant USSD transaction
- Accessibility: Works on any phone, any network, any language
- Trust: Community endorsement model instead of institutional brand

**CREATE:**
- USSD-based micro-lending (no app, no smartphone)
- Merchant trust network (community vouching replaces credit bureau)
- Daily micro-repayment (matches cash flow of market traders)
- Financial literacy via SMS tips (in local language)

---

### Blue Ocean Opportunity Summary

**The opportunity:** Serve 40+ million unbanked Nigerians with a USSD-based micro-lending platform that uses merchant trust networks instead of credit scores, operates in local languages, and matches daily cash flow patterns of market traders.

**Primary customers:** Market traders (Tier 2 refusing non-customers) and rural informal workers (Tier 3 unexplored non-customers)

**Key differentiator:** No smartphone, no documents, no minimum balance, no English required. Community trust replaces institutional verification.

**Cost structure impact:** No branches, no staff-intensive KYC, no app development. USSD is nearly free. Operating cost is a fraction of traditional banking.

**Value creation impact:** Instant access to capital when needed. Payments aligned with income rhythm. Financial literacy building. Community connections strengthened.

## Example 3: Remote Collaboration (Beyond Enterprise)

### Strategy Canvas (abbreviated)

**Competitive Factors:**
Feature richness, integrations, enterprise security, video quality, storage space, admin controls, pricing per seat, onboarding complexity, offline capability, async support

**Observation:** All major players (Slack, Teams, Notion) optimize for enterprise buyers. Value curves nearly identical for the enterprise segment. Nobody optimizes for solo entrepreneurs, freelancers, or informal teams.

### Non-Customer Analysis

**Tier 2: Solo Entrepreneurs / Freelancers**
- Refuse enterprise tools because pricing is per-seat (useless for 1 person)
- Enterprise features (admin controls, compliance) are irrelevant
- Use a cobbled-together stack (WhatsApp + Google Docs + email)
- **Would use:** A simple, cheap, async-first tool designed for one person collaborating with multiple clients

**Tier 3: Informal Teams in Emerging Markets**
- 5-person teams in Southeast Asia using Facebook Messenger for project coordination
- Enterprise tools are too expensive, too complex, and assume reliable internet
- **Would use:** Something that works on mobile, offline-capable, free or very cheap, WhatsApp-like simplicity

### Four Actions Framework (abbreviated)

**ELIMINATE:** Enterprise admin controls, compliance features, complex onboarding
**REDUCE:** Video meeting capability (async first), storage (use links not uploads), pricing
**RAISE:** Mobile experience, offline capability, async communication, simplicity
**CREATE:** WhatsApp-like interface for project collaboration, auto-translation for multilingual teams, pay-per-project pricing (not per-seat)
